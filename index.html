    Each score is the system's estimate of the chance that a deterrent turns that animal back. The small figures are turn-backs over uses. In fixed mode scores stay at the starting 50%.

    • Animal reactions use made-up probabilities. A detected group is simulated as one event, and its members are shown together for illustration. After a failed Level 2 deterrent, there is a 25% chance the group will pause for 30–90 real minutes; at Level 3, the pause is 30–40 real minutes. Each use of a deterrent also lowers its effect on that species for a while, which stands in for habituation. These numbers show the logic and are not measured animal behaviour.
    • A PIR or Doppler sensor does not measure distance. The three levels assume sensor bands, a distance method or camera size estimates, all to be confirmed with real hardware.
    • The pole that detects the animal first owns the event, so overlapping coverage at the corners does not fire two responses.
    • The electric fence is always energised. Check local wildlife and electric fence rules before building it.
